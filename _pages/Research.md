## Work in Progress

**Introducing Flexicurity: Labor Market Effects of an Unemployment Insurance Reform and Severance Pay**
with José Garcia-Louzao, Jonas Jessen, Robin Jessen, and Linas Tarasonis


<details>
<summary><small>Abstract</small></summary>
<small>We study Lithuania's 2017 flexicurity reform, which simultaneously raised unemployment insurance generosity by 50% in both benefit level and potential duration and reduced firing costs through a new labor code. Using administrative data on the universe of Lithuanian workers and difference-in-differences and regression discontinuity designs, we quantify the fiscal cost of more generous unemployment insurance. Exploiting tenure-based variation in severance pay, we separate liquidity effects from moral hazard to assess the insurance value of benefits.</small>
</details>

**Preferences for Government Spending**
with Mark Andor, Robin Jessen, and Johannes König

<details>
<summary><small>Abstract</small></summary>
<small>We quantify the aggregate willingness to pay for additional government spending across nine areas, including education, infrastructure, defense, and transfers. Using a large representative survey experiment in Germany with randomized referendum-style questions, we estimate the distribution of willingness to pay and assess whether spending in each area is too low or too high based on the Samuelson condition. We also document how preferences differ by income, region, political affiliation, and trust in government.</small>
</details>

**Gender Differences in Labor Market Expectations**
with Almut Balleer

<details>
<summary><small>Abstract</small></summary>
<small>We study gender differences in workers' expectations about job loss, job finding, and job-to-job transitions, and compare them with realized outcomes to measure biases in beliefs and the effects on employment and wages.</small>
</details>
